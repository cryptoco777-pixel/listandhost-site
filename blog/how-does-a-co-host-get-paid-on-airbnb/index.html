How Does a Co Host Get Paid on Airbnb? Splits and Fees

List & Host team · October 18, 2026

A co host gets paid on Airbnb when the listing owner turns on payout sharing: Airbnb then pays the co host a cut of every booking, set as a percentage, a fixed amount, the cleaning fee, or the cleaning fee plus a percentage, worked out from the host's earnings after Airbnb's host service fee. For most stays the money reaches the co host's own payout method by the end of the business day after check in, and only the owner can set or change it.

Below: Airbnb's rules (Help Center, read October 18, 2026), a worked split on one real payout from a home our team runs, what co hosts charge, and Vrbo. We offer co hosting, so we have a stake in the answer.

Short answer. The owner sets the split, the co host confirms it, and Airbnb pays co hosts first and the owner what is left. A percentage comes off earnings after Airbnb's fee, so a co host quoted 20% of gross needs a bigger split setting or an invoice. Vrbo has no split: the owner pays a Vrbo helper directly.

Short answer: how the money moves

  1. The guest pays Airbnb one price for the nights and the cleaning fee, plus taxes, under Airbnb's single fee, which hosts who use property management software must use.
  2. Airbnb takes its host service fee: 15.5% for most hosts, typically 14% to 16% for the rest, on the nightly price and fees such as cleaning. The rest is the host's potential earnings.
  3. Co hosts are paid first from those earnings, in a set order. The owner gets what remains.
  4. Airbnb releases the money by the end of the business day after check in. Hosts without two completed stays (unless their listing is verified) and listings with ongoing issues wait until after checkout. Stays of 28 nights or more pay monthly.

Basis: Airbnb Help Center articles on co host payouts, service fees and payout methods, read October 18, 2026.

  • Only the owner sets it. A co host cannot edit a payout, even with full access. They have 14 days to confirm, and a change applies only to bookings that start after they confirm it.
  • Only Airbnb stays are shared. Damage reimbursements and Resolution Center payouts stay with the owner. Two resolution payments, $120 and $50, that reached the Fort Worth home our team runs with one September 2026 stay, one of them for extra pets, would not be split (our booking records).
  • Several co hosts are paid in order, cleaning fee splits first and fixed amounts last. If the earnings run out, the owner is last in line: in Airbnb's own example, four co hosts take $275 of $500 and the owner keeps $225.
  • The co host needs their own payout method, such as a bank account or PayPal, and a verified identity where Airbnb asks.

Putting the cleaning fee inside a 20% split moves $45 to the co host on this one stay. A co host quoted 20% of the $1,646 nightly rent before Airbnb's fee expects $329.20, which no 20% setting pays: it would take about 24.3% with the cleaning fee left out, and the matching number shifts from stay to stay, so many owners pay a gross based fee by invoice instead.

This stay is the clean case. Across 64 Airbnb stays at the three homes our team runs (check ins July 2026 to June 2027, as booked October 16, 2026), Airbnb's fee plus discounts and refunds took 19.9% of what guests were charged at list price. Discounts come out of the earnings a percentage is taken from: an $89.40 last minute discount on a four night Fort Worth stay in October 2026 cut a 20% co host's share by about $15. A fixed amount would not have moved.

Vrbo: users and payouts on a partner account

Vrbo has no co host role and no payout split. Our guide to Airbnb vs Vrbo vs Booking.com compares the three.

  • Access. The owner invites a helper by email from the account settings, with one level of access covering messages, calendar, rates, listing and reservations, while financial settings stay with the owner, per Hostfully (May 25, 2026). Vrbo's own help pages on users did not load for us on October 18, 2026, so check the labels in your dashboard.
  • Payouts. Vrbo pays the payout account set for each listing under Payments, then Payout accounts. It says listings cannot be transferred to another party, and a bank change made because of a new manager may be revoked (Vrbo Help Center, read October 18, 2026).
  • Paying the helper. By invoice, on the base you agreed. At the homes our team runs, Vrbo kept 8.0% of list price across 9 stays, or 11.2% counting the 3.5% payment cut on Vrbo deposits (as booked October 16, 2026), so agree whether the helper's percentage comes before or after that.

Airbnb's Co-Host Network and its bar

Airbnb's directory of co hosts for hire, the Co-Host Network, launched October 16, 2024 with more than 10,000 co hosts in 10 countries, the United States among them. In our search check on October 17, 2026, its Fort Worth page showed three co hosts with ratings and years hosting, but no fees. To join, Airbnb's Help Center (read October 18, 2026) asks for:

  • An active listing as a host, a full access co host or a calendar and messaging co host.
  • In the past 12 months, 10 or more stays, or 3 or more stays totaling 100 or more nights.
  • An average rating of 4.8 or higher over the past 12 months, across every listing they support.
  • Cancellations under 3%, a verified identity, and in the US a personal display name, not a business.

A co host rated below 4.7 may be hidden from the network's search, and below 4.5 Airbnb may end its network agreement. The bar shows a track record. It does not show the fee, the base or who covers a lockout at night, so ask. Our 15 questions to ask an Airbnb manager work for a co host too.

You keep the listing, reviews and payouts

A co host joins your listing; they do not take it. The listing, its reviews and its payout account stay in your name, and removing a co host stops their payouts. Ask anyone you hire whether your home stays on your account. The glass house above is an Airbnb Guest Favorite rated 4.91 from 11 reviews (October 16, 2026), and that rating belongs to the listing, not to whoever runs it. Questions owners ask

How does a co host get paid on Airbnb?

The listing owner sets a payout split in the listing editor: the cleaning fee, the cleaning fee plus a percentage, a percentage, or a fixed amount per booking. Once the co host confirms, Airbnb pays their share from the host's earnings after Airbnb's host service fee.

Can I share the cleaning fee with my co host?

Yes. You can give a co host the full cleaning fee, the cleaning fee plus a percentage of the rest, or a percentage with the cleaning fee in or out. On one $1,580.99 payout at a home our team runs (October 2026), including it in a 20% split moved $45 to the co host.

When does an Airbnb co host get paid?

For most stays, by the end of the business day after check in, alongside the host. Newer hosts and listings with ongoing issues are paid after checkout, and stays of 28 nights or more pay monthly.

What percentage does an Airbnb co host take?

Owner and co host agree on it. 2026 guides from Awning, TIDY and Hostfully put co hosts at about 10% to 25%, usually of gross bookings, while Airbnb's split is taken after its host fee.

Does Vrbo split payouts with a co host?

No. The owner gives a helper access to the account and pays them directly, while Vrbo pays the owner.
