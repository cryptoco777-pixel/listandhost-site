House Not Selling? How to Test It as a Short Term Rental

List & Host team · October 18, 2026

If your house is not selling, a short term rental makes sense only when what it leaves after platform fees, cleaning, utilities and setup beats what the empty house costs you each month, and beats the price cut or lease you would otherwise accept. The way to find out is not a projection but a short, capped test on real bookings: set a budget, list with a short booking window, and judge it on payouts after about 90 days.

Below: the numbers to check before you spend anything, a worked example with the basis for every figure, the risks we see at the homes our team runs in Fort Worth and the Buffalo River region of Arkansas, and a 90 day test plan.

Why so many sellers are asking

Homes are taking longer to sell. Realtor.com's September 2026 housing report put the national median time on market at 61 days, with 20.8% of active listings carrying a price cut. In the Dallas, Fort Worth and Arlington metro, 27.5% of listings had a price cut that month. Redfin data reported by Stacker put Fort Worth's median time on market at an average of 57.7 days for January to June 2026.

Many owners turn to renting. Zillow found that 2.3% of homes listed for rent on its site in October 2025 had recently been listed for sale and not sold, matching its October 2022 level, and its March 2026 list put Dallas at 3.4%. Most of those are long term leases. A short term rental is a different business: more money per night, more work, more cost and more ways to be wrong.

The numbers to check before you spend anything

1. What the empty house costs you each month

This is the bar a rental has to clear. Add your loan payment if there is one, your insurance, the yearly bills that come with owning the house spread over twelve months, basic utilities, lawn care and small repairs. You pay this every month the house sits, whether anyone stays or not.

2. What homes like yours actually book

Start with market data that carries a date, and expect the sources to disagree. For Fort Worth, AirROI puts the average listing at $24,276 a year in bookings (August 2025 to July 2026), while Airbtics puts the median at about $43,000 (September 2025 to August 2026). For the Jasper area of the Buffalo River region, AirROI shows an average of $26,695 a year with 37.3% of nights booked over August 2025 to July 2026. Many listings in those figures are not open every night, and a home can land well above or below them.

Then look at the season. At the 3 bedroom Fort Worth home our team runs, 39% of nights sold in August 2026 and 77% in October, counting October as booked on October 16. AirROI names January, February and September as the weak months in both Fort Worth and the Jasper area. A test that only runs through a strong fall will flatter the numbers. Our Fort Worth income guide and our Buffalo River season guide go month by month, and our Ozark cabin rental guide covers running a cabin from out of town.

3. What comes off the top

  • Platform fees and discounts. Airbnb's single host fee is 15.5% for most hosts (Airbnb Help Center, read October 17, 2026). Across 75 stays at the three homes our team runs, with check ins from July 2026 to June 2027 as booked on October 16, 2026, platform fees and discounts took 18.7% of what guests were charged. On Airbnb alone it was 19.9% over 64 stays.
  • Cleaning. Guests pay a cleaning fee, and it pays the cleaner. At our Fort Worth home guests pay $110 and the cleaner earns $90 a turn (October 2026). Stays there average 2.7 nights (stays booked as of October 16, 2026), so turns add up fast.
  • Utilities. Guests use the house fully. Electricity at our Fort Worth home has run about $200 to $230 a month in 2026. Add internet and streaming.
  • Supplies, repairs and wear. Paper goods and coffee every month, plus what guests break or wear out.
  • Management, if you hire it. Our full service fee is 20% of booking revenue, worked out after platform fees and never charged on cleaning fees or taxes. Our pricing page shows what is billed at cost.

4. What it costs to get guest ready

Awning puts furnishing a typical short term rental at roughly $10,000 to $30,000 (June 2026). RedAwning puts a 3 bedroom house at $15,000 to $35,000, plus $1,000 to $10,000 for linens, toiletries and other guest supplies (updated May 2026). Both sell services to owners, so treat those as vendor ranges. A house that still has your furniture, or is staged, can start for far less: good beds, two sets of linens per bed, towels, kitchen basics and a smart lock. Our guest ready checklist lists what guests expect.

5. How long the first bookings take

A new listing starts with no reviews, and Airbnb shows a star rating only after three. Airbnb lets a new listing offer 20% off its first 3 bookings (Airbnb Help Center, read October 17, 2026). Our cabin loft in the Buffalo River region went live on September 24, 2026, priced at $89 weeknights and $149 weekends until it had three reviews. Its first booking came 3 days later, and it had 6 stays booked by October 14. That was one cabin in one fall. Plan for your first month to be the weakest, not the average.

A worked example

This is an example to show the method, not a forecast for any home. A 3 bedroom Fort Worth house has sat unsold, and holding it costs the owner $2,000 a month (example: loan payment, insurance, yearly ownership bills, basic utilities and lawn). Read the table three ways.

  • Against the holding cost. With full service, an average month leaves $1,590, about 80% of the $2,000 it costs to hold the house. Run yourself, $2,114 covers all of it, before your hours. A February that books $2,700 leaves about $1,290 with full service, and the owner pays the rest.
  • Against the setup. $20,000 takes about 13 months to earn back at $1,590 a month, or about 9 and a half months at $2,114. If you expect to sell within six months, buying all new furniture rarely pays back. A test that uses the furniture already in the house can: $6,000 for beds, linens and supplies earns back in about 4 months at the same pace.
  • Against your other options. Compare what is left, and your hours, with your best written lease quote (no furniture needed) and with what a price cut would cost you.

The risks, plainly

  • Setup money that does not come back. The payback math above is the biggest risk for an owner who still plans to sell soon.
  • A slow first season. New listings start with no reviews, and the weak months in AirROI's data (January, February and September) are weak.
  • More competition every year. AirROI counted 29% more active Fort Worth listings over one year, while its revenue figure rose 2.3% (August 2025 to July 2026). More homes are chasing the same guests.
  • Wear and damage. Hundreds of guest nights a year wear floors, walls and furniture. At our Fort Worth home a $100 damage payment in July 2026 and a $120 damage charge in September 2026 were collected through the platforms. Not every loss comes back.
  • Insurance gaps. The Insurance Information Institute said in March 2026 that standard homeowner policies generally do not cover commercial activity such as short term renting, and that owners who do not tell their insurer risk denied claims. Tell your agent, and get a written quote for a policy that covers paying guests before the first stay.
  • Guests on the books when a buyer appears. If a host cancels a confirmed Airbnb stay, the fee is 10% of the reservation amount more than 30 days before check in, 25% from 48 hours to 30 days, and 50% inside 48 hours, with a $50 minimum, and Airbnb may block those dates (Help Center, read October 17, 2026). A short booking window keeps that risk small.
  • Your time. Hosts in a 2025 PriceLabs survey reported 8.3 hours a week on their listings. Our guide to self managing versus hiring a manager breaks the hours down.

How to test it in 90 days

  1. Write down your bar. Your monthly holding cost, your best written lease quote and the lowest sale price you would take. Decide now what result keeps the test going.
  2. Get a range for the house. Low, likely and high, with the slow months, from a source that shows its basis.
  3. Cap the setup. Use what is already in the house, buy for sleep and photos first, and set a hard budget before the first purchase.
  4. Keep the booking window short. Airbnb lets you open your calendar 3, 6, 9, 12 or 24 months ahead (Help Center, read October 17, 2026). Three months keeps future bookings few if a buyer comes along.
  5. Price for the first three reviews. Start under the market, use the new listing discount, and raise prices as reviews land. Our cabin loft launch shows every price move we made.
  6. Track four numbers every week. Nights booked, average night, payouts after platform fees and costs paid. Our guide to dynamic pricing explains how nightly prices should move.
  7. Decide on day 90 with the scorecard below.

If you sell later, the test is not wasted. Payout reports and reviews give a buyer real numbers instead of a projection, which is what our guide for buyers tells them to ask for. Ninety days is a short record, so expect buyers to weigh it lightly.

Quick answers

Should I rent my house instead of selling it?

It comes down to three numbers: what the empty house costs you each month, what renting would leave after fees and costs, and what a price cut would cost you. Setup costs and slow months can make a short term rental lose money, so run a short, capped test before you commit.

How much does it cost to turn a house into a short term rental?

Awning puts furnishing a typical short term rental at roughly $10,000 to $30,000 (June 2026). RedAwning puts a 3 bedroom house at $15,000 to $35,000 (updated May 2026). A house that still has its furniture can start for far less.

How fast does a new short term rental get its first booking?

It depends on the market and the price. Our cabin loft in the Buffalo River region got its first booking 3 days after going live on September 24, 2026, priced under the market until its first reviews. Plan for the first month to be the slowest.

Can I keep the house listed for sale while it takes guests?

It can be done, but showings and stays compete for the same days. Block showing times on the calendar and keep the booking window short, because cancelling a confirmed Airbnb stay costs the host 10% to 50% of the reservation amount, with a $50 minimum, per Airbnb's Help Center on October 17, 2026.

Is a long term lease better than a short term rental for a house that will not sell?

A lease brings steadier money with less work and no furniture, but a tenant living there makes a quick sale harder. A short term rental can bring more in a good month, with more work, cost and swing by season. Compare both against what the empty house costs you.
