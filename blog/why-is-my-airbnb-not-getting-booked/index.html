Why Is My Airbnb Not Getting Booked? A Page One Check

List & Host team · October 18, 2026

Most of the time, an Airbnb is not getting booked because its total price sits above the homes guests see on page one for the same dates and group size. Check that first, then minimum stays and how much of the market is still open, and only then photos, title, reviews, response time, calendar sync and amenities. Below are all nine checks, with numbers from a live search around the glass house our team runs in the Buffalo River region, including a weekend we priced too high. We run the same checks in Fort Worth.

The short answer: price against page one, then everything else

Guests type a place, dates and a guest count, then compare the totals on the first page. Airbnb says its search weighs your total price against other listings in the area for those dates, and that homes priced below comparable listings tend to rank higher. In the United States, guests see a total that includes fees, before taxes. So what matters is not your nightly rate but the total for the stay, fees included, next to the homes around it. Price is not the only reason a home sits empty, but it is the fastest to check and fix.

Our own over priced weekend

We had Halloween weekend, Friday October 30 to Sunday November 1, at $749 a night. For 8 guests that came to $1,863: $1,498 for two nights, the $225 cleaning fee and $140 for guests 7 and 8. Seventeen days out, our total was above every comparable home on page one and almost double the $975 median, with 52 of 149 homes still open.

Why: the weekend runs into peak fall color, which a public foliage forecast puts around November 4, and two October weekends here had sold at $749 a night. But those were booked in August and September. A price that sold two months ahead is no proof it will sell 17 days ahead.

On October 16 we lowered both nights to $649. With the same fees that is $1,663 before any last minute discount, still above the highest page one total from October 13. On the morning of October 18 both nights were still open. Count fees the way guests do: a $225 cleaning fee adds $112.50 a night to a two night stay.

Check 2: orphan nights and minimum stays

An orphan night is an open night a booking has cut off from its neighbors. Minimum stays can make it impossible to book, and fees can make it impossible to sell.

Our example is Friday, November 20. On September 16 a guest booked four nights starting that Saturday. The Friday could now sell only as one night, or as the end of a stay starting earlier in the week, yet it kept our two night weekend minimum, so for 20 days no guest could arrive that Friday. On October 6 our engine's gap rule cut its minimum to one night.

That fixed the rule, not the price. In our search for that single night, only 9 of 149 homes showed up, against 63 for the full weekend, a sign that most homes nearby want two nights on a Friday. Four comparable homes asked $251 to $331. Ours asked $944: $649 for the night, the full $225 cleaning fee and $70 for guests 7 and 8. Even at this home's $349 floor it would be $644. On October 18 the night was still open at $649.

  • Set a one night minimum on every true gap. Our dynamic pricing guide shows the rule we run.
  • Look at the cleaning fee on short stays. Airbnb lets hosts set a lower cleaning fee for stays of 1 or 2 nights. On our lone Friday the full fee was about a quarter of the total.
  • Offer the night to the guests next to it. In October one guest at this house added the gap night before their arrival. Our glass house case study has the details.
  • Do not cut a whole weekend to sell one stranded night. A big home alone on one night competes with smaller homes, and some single nights stay empty.

Check 3: how much of the market is still open

Airbnb shows how many homes match a search. Run it with your dates and without to see what share of the market is still for sale.

  • Under 10 percent open. On the two regular October weekends, 5 and 9 of 149 homes were open. Sensibly priced homes are selling. If yours is empty, look for what hides it: a minimum no guest can meet, a blocked night, a guest count set too low or a calendar that did not sync.
  • A third or more open. Halloween weekend had 35 percent open and October 27 to 29 had 44 percent. With that many choices, price decides.
  • Far out. About ten weeks ahead, 57 percent of homes were open for Christmas and 54 percent for New Year; six weeks ahead, 20 percent for Thanksgiving. A high share that far out is no reason to cut. Watch how fast it falls week to week.

Open homes also show what a date is worth: in peak color they asked a median of $962.50 for October 27 to 29, midweek, nearly the $975 of Halloween weekend.

And make sure the home really is slow. In AirROI's public data, the average listing in each of the 12 markets our estimator covers was booked 33 to 41 percent of nights from August 2025 to July 2026, and 37.3 percent across 180 listings in the Buffalo River region (read October 16, 2026). Compare your month with your market's month, not with a full calendar. Our glass house booked 68 of 92 nights from August through October 2026 (October as booked on October 16; 53 nights booked before our pricing engine took over on September 21), as our results page shows.

Checks 4 to 9: the listing itself

If your total is inside the page one band and your minimums can be met, look at the listing.

Check 4: photos

The first photo wins the click and the first five keep a guest looking. Lead with what sets the home apart, in daylight. Every photo must match the stay: accuracy is one of the six ratings Airbnb collects, and ratings feed ranking. Our guest ready checklist covers what to finish first.

Check 5: title and first lines

Name the main feature, the setting and who the home suits, not a pile of keywords. Do not expect too much from it: when we checked the glass house's ranking in September 2026, reviews, the Guest Favorite badge and a price inside the band of nearby homes mattered, and the title did not.

Check 6: reviews

A home with few reviews has to win on price until it earns them. Guest Favorite needs at least 5 reviews in the past 4 years, 1 of them in the past 2 years, plus strong ratings and reliability. Fix what guests repeat and never offer anything for a review, which Airbnb's policy forbids. Our glass house had a 4.91 rating from 11 reviews on October 16, 2026. More in our ranking and Guest Favorite guide.

Check 7: response time

Airbnb's response rate is the share of new inquiries and requests you answered within 24 hours over the past 30 days. For homes without Instant Book, Airbnb says search weighs how fast you respond, and it says instantly bookable homes may rank higher. Turn on Instant Book and answer in minutes. In October 2026 the glass house listing showed a 100 percent response rate.

Check 8: calendar sync

Airbnb says linked calendars update every 3 hours, so a night can sell twice inside that window, and a host cancellation counts against ranking and badges. Also look for nights blocked by an old import or a forgotten hold. Our Airbnb, Vrbo and Booking.com guide covers running more than one site.

Check 9: amenities guests filter for

Airbnb's filters include Instant Book, Self check in, Allows pets, Guest favorites and amenity groups from wifi to parking. A home that has not ticked an amenity drops out of every search filtered for it, even if it has one. Guest count works the same way: a home set to sleep 6 never appears in an 8 guest search. Hot tub owners can start with our hot tub guide.

A weekly ten minute routine

  1. Minutes 1 to 4. In a private window, search your next open weekend and one open midweek stay two to five weeks out. Note the median page one total and where yours sits.
  2. Minutes 5 and 6. Note how many homes are still open, against last week.
  3. Minute 7. Find orphan nights in the next 60 days and check each minimum stay.
  4. Minute 8. Compare calendars across every site.
  5. Minutes 9 and 10. Read new reviews and questions, and fix one thing.

Then decide. Above three quarters of page one with a third of the market open: move the price, never below your floor. Inside the band with the market nearly sold out: find what hides the home. Log the totals weekly, so you act on a trend.

Questions hosts ask

Why is my Airbnb getting views but no bookings?

Views mean the home shows up in search. No bookings usually means it loses the comparison with the homes beside it, most often on total price, then photos or reviews.

How long should I wait before lowering my price?

If a third or more of comparable homes are still open two to three weeks out and your total is above most of page one, move now. If under 10 percent are open, price is rarely the problem. Keep a floor either way. Those are our thresholds, drawn from the October 13, 2026 search above.

Does a lower price help my Airbnb ranking?

Airbnb says homes priced below comparable listings in the area for the same dates tend to rank higher. Price is one factor among several, with quality, availability, Instant Book and response speed.

Should I drop my minimum stay?

On true gap nights, yes, to one night. Elsewhere a two night weekend minimum keeps a single Saturday booking from stranding the Friday. Make sure no rule leaves a night that no stay can reach.
