Airbnb vs Vrbo vs Booking.com for Owners: Fees Compared

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

Most owners start on Airbnb, then wonder whether Vrbo and Booking.com are worth the extra work. The honest answer: usually yes, but not for the reasons people expect. The extra channels rarely double your bookings. They fill gaps, reach guests who never open Airbnb, and protect you if one platform changes its rules. Here is how the three compare in 2026, with what bookings at the homes our team runs show. Our team runs homes this way in Fort Worth and the Buffalo River region, and offers pricing and listing work to owners anywhere through our services.

What each platform charges

PlatformWhat the host paysWhat the guest pays on top
AirbnbA single host fee, 15.5% for most hosts. Airbnb is moving hosts off the old split fee, where the host paid about 3% and the guest paid a separate fee.Under the single fee, the whole fee comes out of the host payout, so the guest sees no separate service fee.
VrboPay per booking: a 5% commission on the rent and required fees, plus 3% payment processing on what the guest pays.A service fee on a sliding scale.
Booking.comA commission set in your agreement, typically around 15%. It can vary with location and the options you choose.In our bookings, no separate guest fee beyond taxes.

Vrbo also offers an annual subscription in place of pay per booking for some hosts. Check each platform's current terms before you list, because fees change.

Booking subtotal means the nightly rate plus the cleaning and other fees, after any discounts. On our Vrbo stays, guests also paid a Vrbo service fee of about 12.8% on top of that subtotal. Shares use the booking revenue in our booking system. Counted by bank deposits instead, Airbnb was about 85%, Vrbo about 13% and Booking.com about 1%, mostly because one Vrbo stay was partly refunded to the guest.

Three things stand out:

  • Airbnb is the engine. It brought more than four of every five dollars across the homes our team runs.
  • Vrbo adds real money. About 15% of booking revenue. Our Vrbo stays ran a little longer and a little larger, though 9 stays is a small sample. At the glass house, Vrbo brought 6 stays, including a 5 night fall stay and an 8 guest summer weekend.
  • Booking.com is small but real. Two stays so far: three late September weeknights at the glass house, and a November concert weekend in Fort Worth that sold within hours of a price change.

The fee math, side by side

This is an illustration. Your real rates depend on your agreements, your market and the options you turn on. Vrbo's processing fee is charged on the full amount the guest pays, taxes included, so it runs a little higher than shown.

Vrbo looks cheapest for the host, but its guest fee makes the same home look more expensive to the guest. When you set prices, compare what the guest sees on each site, not only what you keep. What matters in the end is what you keep per night, after fees, across the whole year.

How the platforms behave differently

Cancellations

Each platform has its own cancellation policies, and you set them separately on each one. At the glass house our team runs, 7 of 35 Airbnb bookings were cancelled before arrival in our first months, all made while the listing had a moderate policy that refunded in full until five days before arrival. We moved it to a firm policy. On Vrbo, the same home uses a strict policy with a full refund only 60 or more days before arrival. Choose the policy that fits your market, then check it on every channel.

Search and ranking

Each platform ranks listings with its own signals, such as reviews, price, response speed, cancellations and how often the listing books. Reviews do not carry across. A home with 50 Airbnb reviews starts at zero on Vrbo. Expect a slow first few months on each new channel, and price a little sharper until the first reviews land.

Double bookings: the one risk you must solve

Listing on three sites means three calendars. If they are not in sync, two guests can book the same night, and one of them gets cancelled. Basic calendar links between sites can lag. A channel manager or property management system that connects to each platform directly keeps prices, minimum stays and availability in one place. Our team runs its homes this way: one calendar, one price writer, and every price change read back to confirm it landed. You can see what that system does on our results page.

Our recommendation

  1. Start on Airbnb and earn your first reviews there. That is where most guests are.
  2. Add Vrbo once the listing is stable, especially for homes that sleep six or more.
  3. Add Booking.com when your calendar sync is solid and your house rules are written down.
  4. Write each listing for its own site. Never mention one platform inside another platform's listing, and keep contact details inside each platform's messages.
  5. Compare by what you keep. Every quarter, look at payout per night after fees for each channel.

Questions owners ask

Is Vrbo cheaper than Airbnb for hosts?

On the host side, usually yes: Vrbo's pay per booking terms are a 5% commission plus 3% payment processing, against Airbnb's 15.5% single fee for most hosts. But Vrbo adds a service fee for the guest, so the same home can look more expensive to guests on Vrbo.

Should I list on all three platforms?

Most homes do best on Airbnb and Vrbo, with Booking.com added once calendars are synced and rules are written. In our records, Airbnb brought 83% of booking revenue, Vrbo 15% and Booking.com about 2%.

How do I avoid double bookings across platforms?

Use a channel manager or property management system that connects to each platform directly, so one calendar controls availability, prices and minimum stays everywhere.
