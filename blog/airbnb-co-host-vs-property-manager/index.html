Airbnb Co Host vs Property Manager in DFW: Costs and Duties

List & Host team · October 18, 2026

An Airbnb co host joins your listing and runs the work you hand over, usually guest messages, pricing and the calendar, for a fee that 2026 industry guides put at about 10% to 25% of booking revenue, while you keep the listing, the reviews and anything that needs hands at the home. A full service property manager runs all of it, cleaners, repairs and supplies included, for about 18% to 30% in the same guides. In Dallas Fort Worth, which one fits depends less on that gap than on how far you live from the home, how often it turns over and who answers when something breaks at night.

We are a management company that offers both, so we have a stake in your answer. Every outside number below names its source and date, and every number of ours says what it is based on. Checked October 17, 2026.

Short answer. Pick a co host if you live near the home, have a cleaner you trust and the home books longer stays. Pick full service if you live out of state, the home turns over every few nights, or you want Vrbo and Booking.com run too. On $25,000 to $60,000 a year in booking revenue, roughly the span from the average DFW listing to the top 10% in data services' 2026 figures, a co host at 15% and a manager at 20% are about $1,250 to $3,000 a year apart.

What an Airbnb co host does, and what Airbnb lets one do

A co host is a person or a company you add to your Airbnb listing, at one of three access levels you choose. Full access covers the calendar, pricing, listing details, reservations and guest reviews. Calendar and messaging lets them message guests and see the calendar, not change it. Calendar is view only. Only the listing owner can set up or change what a co host is paid.

Airbnb can split each payout for you: the cleaning fee, the cleaning fee plus a percentage, a percentage of each booking, or a fixed amount per booking. The split is worked out from the host's earnings after Airbnb's host fee and covers Airbnb stays only. Vrbo has no co host role and no payout split, so a Vrbo helper gets manager access to your account and you pay them yourself (Hostfully, May 2026).

Airbnb launched its Co-Host Network in October 2024 with more than 10,000 co hosts in 10 countries, including the United States. Airbnb said they averaged 4.9 stars, against 4.62 for property managers with more than 30 active listings, as of September 30, 2024, and footnoted that the rating reflects guest reviews of the listings, not the co host's own work. We are a small team, not one of those large managers, but the lesson holds: size does not earn reviews. Attention does.

Co hosts come in three shapes: a local host who co hosts on the side and often answers fast (ask who covers when they travel), a co hosting company that runs messages, pricing and the cleaning schedule, and a remote co host who runs messages and pricing from anywhere while everything at the door stays with your own people.

What a full service property manager does

A full service manager takes the whole job: the listing and photos, daily pricing, every guest message, cleaners scheduled and checked after each stay, restocking, repairs by local pros, damage claims, reviews and a monthly statement. You approve the big decisions, such as your lowest nightly price and any repair over an agreed amount. Our checklist of what a management company should do lists every item, so you can hold any manager to it.

Two things differ a lot between managers. If the listing moves into the manager's account, the reviews and booking history usually stay with them when you leave. And some managers collect guest payments and pay you later, while others let the platforms pay you directly. Either can work if your statement ties every payout to a booking.

The percentage is only part of the cost. Ask about:

  • Setup fees. TIDY's 2026 guide puts onboarding at $500 to $2,000 per home. Some managers charge nothing.
  • Markups. Repairs are billed at vendor cost, sometimes plus a coordination markup that varies by company, per Awning. Ask about supplies too.
  • The base. The same 20% can be thousands of dollars apart depending on whether it is taken before or after platform fees and whether cleaning fees count. Our guide to management fees works through an example.

In both models, guests usually pay for cleaning through the cleaning fee. At the 3 bedroom Fort Worth home our team runs, guests pay $110 and the cleaner is paid $90 a turnover (our listing and our cleaner's rate, October 2026).

What does the gap buy? Mostly the hands on work a co host usually leaves with you. Our estimate for one home with about eight stays a month, from our self managing guide: restocking 2 to 4 hours a month, maintenance 2 to 6, and problems such as lockouts 1 to 5. That is 60 to 180 hours a year, so on the $40,000 example the $2,000 gap works out to about $11 to $33 an hour. For scale, PriceLabs' 2025 survey of more than 1,400 hosts found 8.3 hours a week spent on listings in total.

If you live 15 minutes away and enjoy the work, that is a fair trade to keep. If you live in another state, you cannot do those hours at all, and paying a local handy person and someone to restock usually closes most of the gap.

Why DFW changes the answer

  • Turnovers set the workload. Every new stay means a clean, a restock and a check. AirROI puts the average stay at 6.8 nights in Fort Worth, 6.2 in Arlington and 5.9 in Dallas (August 2025 to July 2026). The Fort Worth home our team runs near Dickies Arena averages 2.7 nights (every stay booked through June 2027, as of October 16, 2026). At 18 booked nights a month, a 6 night average means about 3 turnovers and a 2.7 night average about 7. A home that hosts traveling nurses for 30 nights suits a co host. A home that sells two night event weekends is a turnover business.
  • Event dates stack the hardest days. The 2027 Fort Worth Stock Show and Rodeo runs January 15 to February 6, and AT&T Stadium hosts the Big 12 Championship on December 4, 2026. Those weeks bring back to back stays and same day turns, priced months ahead. See our guide to Stock Show and Dickies Arena weekends.
  • Guests book late. Dallas stays are booked 30 days ahead on average (AirROI, August 2025 to July 2026). At our Fort Worth home, weeknight only stays were booked a median of one day before arrival (August to October 2026). A remote co host can answer that guest. Only someone local can get the home ready by afternoon.
  • The metroplex is wide. Local in DFW can mean an hour in traffic. Ask where the cleaners and vendors are based, and how fast someone reaches your door when the air conditioner quits on a July night.
  • More homes chase the same guests. AirROI counted 29% more Fort Worth listings than a year earlier, while average revenue rose 2.3% (August 2025 to July 2026). Ask either model how often prices change. Our dynamic pricing guide shows what good looks like.

Who a co host fits

  • You live in DFW, close enough to reach the home in about 30 minutes, or you have a handy person who is.
  • You have a cleaner you trust, and a backup for the weeks they are away.
  • The home books longer stays, so it turns over a few times a month, not a few times a week.
  • You list only on Airbnb, or you are happy to run Vrbo yourself.
  • You want the listing and reviews in your name and the lowest fee, and you are fine filling the gaps.

Who a full service manager fits

  • You live out of state or travel often, and nobody near the home can handle a lockout or a leak.
  • The home turns over often: two and three night stays, game days and event weekends.
  • You own two or more homes, or plan to.
  • You want Airbnb, Vrbo and Booking.com run from one synced calendar, with no double bookings.
  • You want the income without a second job, and one statement a month.

Neither fits everyone. If you live close and like hosting, running it yourself can be the best deal of all. If pricing is your only weak spot, remote pricing costs less than either. Questions owners ask

What is the difference between an Airbnb co host and a property manager?

A co host joins your Airbnb listing with the access you choose, and you keep the listing, the reviews and the payouts. A full service property manager runs the whole operation, cleaners, repairs and supplies included, usually on every booking site. Many co hosts cover messages and pricing only.

How much does an Airbnb co host charge in DFW?

Owner and co host agree on it. We found no published survey for DFW alone. 2026 industry guides put co host fees at about 10% to 25% of booking revenue nationally, up to 30% with cleaning and repairs. On $40,000 a year in booking revenue, 15% is $6,000.

How much does a full service property manager charge?

Fee guides published in 2026 put full service at about 18% to 30% of booking revenue, up to 35% in some markets, with setup fees and markups sometimes on top. Ask each manager what the percentage is taken from.

How does a co host get paid on Airbnb?

The listing owner sets it up: the cleaning fee, the cleaning fee plus a percentage, a percentage of each booking, or a fixed amount per booking, worked out from the host's earnings after Airbnb's host fee. Vrbo has no built in payout split, so the owner pays a Vrbo helper directly.

Is a co host or a property manager better for an out of state owner?

Usually a full service manager, or a co host paired with a local cleaner and handy person you trust. Messages and pricing can run from anywhere. Lockouts, leaks and same day turnovers cannot.
