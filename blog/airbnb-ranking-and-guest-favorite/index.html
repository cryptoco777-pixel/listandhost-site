Airbnb Ranking Basics and How to Earn Guest Favorite

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

Guests choose from the homes Airbnb shows them first. If yours is not among them, nothing else about the home matters: not the hot tub, not the linens, not the price. This guide covers what Airbnb says it ranks on, the real rules for the Guest Favorite badge and for Superhost, and the habits that move a listing up. It applies in any market. Our team runs the same playbook on the homes it runs in Fort Worth and the Buffalo River region, and offers it to owners anywhere through our remote pricing and listing service.

What Airbnb says it ranks on

Airbnb publishes the main factors behind its search results in its help center. In plain words:

  • Quality. Photos, ratings and reviews, how you communicate with guests, listing details, customer service history, cancellations and amenities. Airbnb says higher quality listings with better ratings and reviews tend to rank higher.
  • Popularity. How often guests save your listing to a wishlist, message you and book.
  • Price. Your total price compared with similar listings nearby. Airbnb says listings priced below comparable listings in the area tend to rank higher.
  • Location. Homes in places guests like to stay, such as near popular landmarks, can rank higher.
  • Availability. More open dates and more flexible stay lengths help a listing match more trips.
  • Instant Book. Instantly bookable listings may rank higher because the booking is confirmed right away.
  • Responsiveness. For listings that are not instantly bookable, how quickly you answer inquiries and how often you decline requests.
  • Personalization and variety. A guest's past bookings shape what they see, and Airbnb mixes in different hosts and price points.

New listings are designed to show up well at first and usually appear in search within 24 hours of going live. That early window is the best chance a home gets, so open the calendar only when the home is fully ready. Our guest ready checklist covers what to finish before launch.

What Guest Favorite is

Airbnb launched Guest Favorites on November 8, 2023, as a collection of about 2 million of its most loved homes. At launch, Airbnb said these homes were rated above 4.9 stars on average and scored high in six categories: ease of check in, cleanliness, accuracy, host communication, location and value. It said host cancellations and quality issues for these homes averaged about 1 percent.

The current rules in Airbnb's help center:

  • At least 5 reviews in the past 4 years, with at least 1 of them in the past 2 years.
  • The overall star rating plus the six category ratings.
  • Reliability: the host cancellation rate, how often reviews get removed, and quality incidents reported to Airbnb customer service.
  • Homes are compared globally, within their country, or both.
  • Eligible homes are evaluated every day. The top 1, 5 and 10 percent also get a gold trophy, a gold badge and a highlight at the top of the listing.

Airbnb does not publish one cutoff score. In practice, every review below five stars weighs a lot while your count is small. With five reviews, a single four star review pulls the average down to 4.8. With fifty reviews, the same review barely moves it. That is why the first months of a listing deserve the most care.

Superhost is a separate badge

Guest Favorite belongs to a home. Superhost belongs to the host. Airbnb checks Superhost status every three months, looking back over the past 12 months, and awards it four times a year. To qualify, a host needs:

  • A 4.8 or higher overall rating.
  • Replies to 90 percent of new messages, and an accept or decline on new requests, within 24 hours.
  • A cancellation rate under 1 percent.
  • At least 10 reservations, or 3 reservations that total at least 100 nights.

Only the owner of a listing is evaluated, not co hosts. That matters if you hire help. In a co hosting setup where you keep the listing, the reviews and the badges stay with your account.

Airbnb also says that for homes, its search looks at the Superhost criteria themselves: host cancellations, responsiveness, and ratings and reviews. So even before you earn the badge, the habits behind it already count.

Nine habits that move a listing up

  1. Launch ready, priced for the first five reviews. A home that opens half finished collects its worst reviews when they count most. When we launched our cabin loft, Airbnb's new listing discount of 20 percent was running, and we kept weeknights modest until the first reviews came in.
  2. Turn on Instant Book. Airbnb says instantly bookable listings may rank higher. Use house rules and guest requirements to screen, not manual approval.
  3. Answer fast, every time. Superhost needs 90 percent of messages answered within 24 hours. Aim for minutes during the day. Guests who get a fast answer book, and booking is a popularity signal.
  4. Keep the calendar open and flexible. Availability and flexible stay lengths are ranking factors. Open nights a year ahead, and let a one night gap between two bookings sell as a one night stay. Our dynamic pricing guide shows how we handle gap nights.
  5. Price against real comparable homes. Airbnb compares your total price, cleaning fee included, with similar homes nearby. Check the homes that show next to yours for the same dates and guest count.
  6. Never cancel on a guest. Host cancellations count against Superhost, against Guest Favorite reliability and against ranking. Keep your calendar synced across every platform so a double booking cannot happen.
  7. Make the listing match the stay. Accuracy is one of the six ratings. If a feature depends on the weather or the season, say so. A guest who finds less than the photos promised rates accuracy low, even if everything else was perfect.
  8. Fix small problems before they become reviews. Send a short message the first morning of each stay. A slow drain fixed on day one is a five star stay. Found on day three, it is a four star review.
  9. Follow up after every stay, honestly. Thank the guest, leave them a review, and ask them to message you first if anything fell short. Never offer anything for a review. Airbnb's review policy says reviews may not be provided or withheld in exchange for something of value, including a discount or a refund.

What does not work

  • Stuffing the title with keywords. Guests search by place, dates and guest count, then filter for amenities. A clear title that names the main feature does more.
  • Trading or paying for reviews. It breaks Airbnb's policy and puts the listing at risk.
  • Pressuring guests to change a review. Airbnb's policy also bans pressure and coordination around reviews.
  • Pricing far below the market for good. Price helps ranking, but a home that stays cheap trains the market to expect it. Use floors and event pricing so a slow week does not set your value.
  • Blocking most of the calendar. Fewer open dates means fewer searches your home can match.

A ranking check you can run every week

  1. Open a private browser window so you are not logged in.
  2. Search your area with real dates two weeks and five weeks out, and a realistic guest count.
  3. Write down your position, the price Airbnb shows for your home, and the prices of the homes around it.
  4. Repeat for one weekend and one weeknight.
  5. If you are off page one, check price first, then photos, then minimum stays and open dates.

Quick answers

How many reviews does a home need for Guest Favorite?

Airbnb requires at least 5 reviews in the past 4 years, with at least 1 of them in the past 2 years. Ratings, the six category scores and reliability decide the rest.

Is Guest Favorite the same as Superhost?

No. Guest Favorite belongs to a home and is evaluated every day. Superhost belongs to the host and is checked every three months over the past 12 months.

Does a lower price help my ranking?

Airbnb says listings priced below comparable homes nearby tend to rank higher. Price is one factor among many, so use price floors to keep a slow week from selling your home too cheap.
