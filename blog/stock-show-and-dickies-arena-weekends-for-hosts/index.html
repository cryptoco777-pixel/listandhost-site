Stock Show and Dickies Arena Weekends: A Host Playbook

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

In Fort Worth, a few dozen dates a year decide whether a short term rental has a good year or an average one. Most of them sit on two calendars: the Fort Worth Stock Show and Rodeo, and the shows at Dickies Arena and the Will Rogers Memorial Center next door. This is how we plan for those dates at the 3 bedroom home our team runs, with the numbers we saw and the mistakes we made. It is the playbook behind our Fort Worth Airbnb management.

Here is what density looks like. Dickies Arena has Morrissey on October 29, Teddy Swims on October 30 and MercyMe on November 1, 2026: three shows across one Halloween weekend. The next weekend, John Summit plays November 7 and 8. Then Hot Wheels Monster Trucks Live runs November 13 to 15. When the arena calendar looks like that, nearby homes should not be priced like an ordinary weekend.

What the Stock Show looks like from the booking side

The Stock Show runs every day for 23 days, so it lifts weeknights too, not only weekends. The show's own visitor page warns that parking at Will Rogers and Dickies Arena can fill early on Saturdays and Sundays. Guests who stay nearby skip that problem, and many will pay for it.

On October 13, 2026, three months before opening, we checked live Airbnb prices for the Stock Show's opening weekend, Friday January 15 to Sunday January 17, 2027. We looked at 14 comparable homes with 3 or more bedrooms for 6 guests in the neighborhoods around the venues.

The home our team runs priced the weekend at $379 a night with a 2 night minimum, which put the total with cleaning at $868, a little above the median. Event weekends book early. Weeknights book late.

At our Fort Worth home, stays that included a Friday or Saturday night were booked a median of 44 days before arrival. Stays with only weeknights were booked a median of 1 day before. That gap shapes the whole playbook: get the weekend price right early, and keep weeknights flexible.

  • A September weekend booked 61 days ahead at $390 a night, the highest average night the home has sold.
  • A 3 night weekend in October, a big football weekend in the area, sold at $254 a night after discounts. We had set a 3 night minimum for those dates, so the weekend could not be broken up by a one night stay.

Mistakes we made, so you do not have to

We let weekends sell too cheap. Two fall weekends at the home our team runs booked weeks ahead at about $209 and $248 a night. One of them overlaps a four day auction at Dickies Arena. By mid October, the comparable homes still open on those weekends asked a median of $1,618 and $1,010 for the stay. Late leftovers tend to be the pricier homes, so the real gap was smaller. But the lesson held, and we raised 23 future weekend nights the same day.

We let automatic discounts slide an event weekend. Our fill rules lower prices on open nights as arrival gets closer. The Friday and Saturday nights of the John Summit weekend, November 6 and 7, had slid to $202 a night before our comp check caught it. Similar homes were asking $773 and up for those two nights. We set it back to $249 with a 2 night minimum on October 9, and it sold on Booking.com about four hours later. Comparable homes asked a median of $704 for that two night stay; ours came to about $567 with cleaning. It sold, but it sold cheap. We now lock event weeks so fill rules cannot touch them. You can see the full story on our Fort Worth results page.

Platform discounts stack. When we checked Halloween weekend, a 15% last minute discount on Airbnb was taking a $329 nightly price down to about $280 for guests. If you set an event floor, check what guests actually see after every discount.

The playbook we use

  1. Load the calendars six months out. The Stock Show schedule, the Dickies Arena events page, the Will Rogers calendar, TCU's football schedule and the holidays.
  2. Set a floor for every event night. A floor is the lowest price that night may ever sell for. Automatic tools may push above it, never below.
  3. Set minimum stays by event. Two nights for most event weekends. Three for the biggest. Once the weekend books, open any one night gaps around it to one night stays.
  4. Check comps every week. Look at what similar homes ask for the same dates, and how many are still open. When fewer than one in four homes are left, hold your price or raise it.
  5. Price Stock Show weeknights as event nights. The show runs daily, so a Tuesday in late January is not an ordinary Tuesday.
  6. Watch discounts. Early bird, weekly, monthly and last minute discounts stack. Know the final price a guest sees.
  7. Prepare the house for crowds. Clear guest limits, parking instructions, quiet hours, and a house manual that answers event questions: where to park, how far to the arena, where to eat late.
  8. Line up cleaners for Sunday. Event weekends end with Sunday checkouts and often Sunday arrivals. Book your cleaner and a backup before the dates sell.

A note for owners who live nearby

Many Fort Worth owners hold back a few event weekends for their own family. That is fine, but block those dates early and on every channel. A last minute owner block on a weekend that already sold means a host cancellation, which can bring platform penalties and cost you the trust of future guests.

Questions owners ask

When should I set prices for the Fort Worth Stock Show?

Set them as soon as the dates are published, usually months ahead. At the home our team runs, weekend stays were booked a median of 44 days before arrival, and the best event weekends often go earlier than that.

Should I require a minimum stay for event weekends?

Usually yes. We use two nights for most event weekends and three for the biggest, then allow one night stays to fill any gap left around a booking.

Do weeknights sell during the Stock Show?

The show runs every day for 23 days, so weeknights carry more demand than usual. We price Stock Show weeknights at $299, against $149 to $199 on ordinary weeknights. That is our asking price, not a result yet.
