Vacasa, Evolve or a Local Manager? Fees and Facts, 2026

List & Host team · October 18, 2026

Before you compare percentages, compare two other things: what each fee is taken from, and whose account holds your listing if you ever leave. As of October 18, 2026, Evolve publishes 10% or 15% of each stay’s rent plus cleaning and pet fees, Vacasa publishes no rate and quotes each home, and our full service fee is 20% of nightly rent plus extra guest fees, after the platform’s host fee and never on cleaning fees.

We are one of the choices in this guide, so we have a stake in your answer. Every figure about another company is its own published claim, read October 18, 2026 unless another date is given, and linked below. We cover fees, terms and listings only, not anyone’s service.

Short answer. Line up three things from every quote: the rate, the base and who holds the listing. On a 3 bedroom Fort Worth home in our estimator’s likely case, Evolve’s 15% plan and our 20% land $150 to $717 apart, and which costs less depends on how many cleaning fees the year holds. The bigger difference comes when you leave: a listing in your own account keeps its reviews.

What changed at Vacasa in 2025

Casago, a vacation rental manager built on a franchise model, bought Vacasa. The purchase closed on April 30, 2025, at $5.30 a share, according to Vacasa’s SEC filing dated May 1, 2025 and Short Term Rentalz. The brand continues as Vacasa, a Casago company (VRMA Arrival, May 5, 2025). Two later facts matter more to an owner:

  • Your agreement. Casago’s page for Vacasa owners says the merger closing does not change the management agreement, and homes stay on the same booking sites.
  • Who runs your market. On August 21, 2026, Short Term Rentalz reported that Casago had sold all former Vacasa markets. They are now led by local franchise owners, destination based businesses or larger regional managers, with the last handovers due in September 2026.

If your home is with Vacasa today, ask who your agreement is with now, whose accounts hold your listings, and whether the fee or notice changed in the handover.

What Vacasa publishes about owner fees

As of October 18, 2026, Vacasa does not publish a rate. Its fee page says its management fees are “tailored for each unique vacation rental property,” and its owner FAQ says the fee depends on the home’s location, number of bedrooms and guest amenities, with a quote from a local market expert.

  • The range on the page is the industry’s. The fee page tells owners to expect 10% to 50% of revenue across vacation rental managers, not as Vacasa’s own rate.
  • Fee base. Neither page says what the fee is taken from. Ask for it in one written sentence.
  • One owner fee, plus guest fees. Vacasa describes one management fee for its core services. Guests pay a booking fee, a cleaning fee, pet and hot tub fees where they apply, and a damage waiver of up to $25 a night in most markets.
  • Notice. The FAQ says you can cancel at any time with 90 days notice, and that stays already booked in that 90 day window are kept.

What Evolve publishes, and what stays with the owner

Evolve publishes its plans, as read October 18, 2026: Core at 10%, Plus at 15% and Pro at a custom rate set by how many homes you have, with a one time onboarding fee of $250 plus $25 for each added property. Its FAQ says the fee is charged after guests check in, on each reservation’s base rate plus additional fees such as cleaning or pet fees, with taxes left out. Those pages do not mention platform fees, so ask whether the base is before or after them. What stays with you, by Evolve’s own description:

  • Cleaners. Evolve connects you with local cleaning partners, or you use your own. You agree the price and pay them from the guest cleaning fee, and Evolve coordinates the schedule.
  • Help at the door. During stays, on the ground support comes from a Guest Contact you choose.
  • Your old listings. Evolve’s help center says listings you already had must be hidden or removed before your Evolve listing launches, as its agreement requires. It does not say whose account the new listing sits in.

Evolve says there are no long term contracts, and offers a refund of the first six months of management fees, requested between day 180 and day 210, with conditions. We found no notice period on the pages we read.

Tech led local models, Awning style

Some newer managers sell city by city from one national platform, with plan tiers and a low starting rate. Awning is one. Its Fort Worth and Dallas pages carry “From 10%” in the title, and their FAQ says full service starts at 10% of monthly revenue, depending on property type, location and services. Another line on the same pages says 15%, and a comparison table shows 5%. The FAQ lists no setup fees and 90 days notice to cancel. Its fee guide, updated October 1, 2026, lists Essential at 10% to 15% and Full Service at 18% to 25%.

A starting rate belongs to the smallest plan, so ask which plan covers cleaning, repairs and restocking, and how the agreement defines revenue. Awning’s own guide shows why: on $60,000 a year at 20%, taking the fee before or after Airbnb’s 15.5% host fee moves the cost by $1,860. Would rather keep the hands on work? Leaving any manager: listing, reviews, future bookings

The exit is harder to compare than the fee, and it decides what you keep. Airbnb’s help center says there is no way to transfer an account to a different host, and reservations cannot be moved to a new host or account. So the account that holds your listing keeps its reviews and future bookings. Vacasa’s own guide for owners who sell says neither Airbnb nor Vrbo lets a host transfer a listing, though you can ask to have reviews moved, and guests asked to rebook on a new listing may not.

Notice, as published: Vacasa 90 days, with stays booked in that window kept; Awning 90 days; Evolve none found. Ours is quoted in writing after a free look, and stays already booked past the end date are honored.

Before you sign with anyone, get in writing:

  1. Whose account holds the listing on Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com and any direct booking site.
  2. What happens to the reviews when you leave, platform by platform.
  3. Who hosts, and who is paid for, stays booked past the end date.
  4. The notice in days, how it must be sent, and any fee to leave early.
  5. Whether your current listing must come down first, and what happens to its bookings.

Questions owners ask

Does Vacasa publish its management fee?

No. As of October 18, 2026, Vacasa quotes each home, with a fee based on location, bedrooms and guest amenities. The 10% to 50% range on its fee page describes vacation rental managers in general, not its own rate.

What does Evolve charge owners in 2026?

As published on October 18, 2026: Core at 10%, Plus at 15% and Pro at a custom rate, plus a one time $250 onboarding fee and $25 for each added property. The fee is taken from the base rate plus fees such as cleaning or pet fees, with taxes left out.

Is Vacasa still Vacasa after the Casago deal?

The brand continues as a Casago company. The purchase closed on April 30, 2025, and on August 21, 2026 Short Term Rentalz reported that Casago had sold all former Vacasa markets to local franchise owners and other operators.

Do I keep my reviews if I leave a manager?

Only if the listing sits in your own account. Airbnb says accounts cannot be transferred to a different host and reservations cannot move to a new account. If the manager's account holds the listing, plan on starting a new one.

Is a lower percentage always cheaper?

No. The base matters as much as the rate. On our Fort Worth example, 15% of rent plus cleaning fees cost $150 less than 20% of rent after Airbnb's host fee when stays averaged 6.8 nights, and $717 more when they averaged 2.7 nights (published rates read October 18, 2026).
