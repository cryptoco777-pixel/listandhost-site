How Much Can an Airbnb Make in Fort Worth? 2026 Numbers

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

Fort Worth is one of the busiest short term rental markets in Texas. Visit Fort Worth counted 11.5 million visitors in 2024. The Stock Show, Dickies Arena, TCU and the Stockyards pull guests all year. But a busy city does not tell you what one home earns. This guide puts three sets of numbers side by side: two national data services, and one real Fort Worth home our team runs. Then it shows how to build an honest estimate for your own place. If you want the short version for your address, our Fort Worth Airbnb management page has a free estimate form.

Short answer. Data services put the typical Fort Worth listing at about $24,000 to $43,000 a year in booking revenue, depending on who is counting and how. A well run 3 bedroom home near the event venues can beat the typical listing, but winter weeks and slow weeknights pull the year down. Every figure below says where it came from.

What the data services say

Two services that track Airbnb listings published Fort Worth numbers this year. They do not agree, and that is worth knowing before you trust any single figure.

Why so far apart? Each service decides which listings count, how to treat blocked nights, and whether to report an average or a median. One very expensive estate pulls an average up. Hundreds of private rooms and small apartments pull it down. AirROI also reports that the number of Fort Worth listings grew 29% in a year, while its revenue figure rose only 2.3%. More homes are chasing the same guests, so pricing, photos and reviews matter more every year.

The services also disagree on the best season. Airbtics says demand peaks in October and November and is weakest in August. AirROI names March, May and June as its peak months and January, February and September as its low ones. The fall at the Fort Worth home our team runs was strong, which fits the first view, but one fall is not a trend.

What one real Fort Worth home booked

Our team runs a 3 bedroom home in west Fort Worth that sleeps 8. It is a short drive from Dickies Arena, TCU and the Stockyards. Its first stay was July 2, 2026, and it is listed on Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com and a direct booking site. Here is what it did from August through October, as of October 16, 2026.

Across those three months the home was booked 56 of 92 nights, or 61%, and paid out $11,706. That is about $127 for every night on the calendar, booked or not. We are not turning that into a yearly number. Fall has been the home's strongest stretch so far, and in mid October its November and December calendars were only 23% and 29% booked. The average night also came down as the calendar filled, because quiet weeknights now sell near a $149 floor instead of sitting empty. Payouts still went up every month.

Weekends and weeknights are two different businesses

The biggest lesson from our first months: weekends and weeknights behave in opposite ways.

  • Price. From August through October, weekend nights (Friday and Saturday) averaged $236 at the home our team runs. Weeknights averaged $139. Both are what guests paid per night, after discounts, before cleaning, taxes and platform fees.
  • Timing. Stays that included a weekend night were booked a median of 44 days ahead. Stays with weeknights only were booked a median of 1 day ahead.

So weekends need the right price early, before the good ones are gone. Weeknights need fast replies, a clean home ready on short notice, and a price that still makes sense the day before.

We learned the weekend half the hard way. On October 13 our weekly comp check found that two fall weekends at the home our team runs had sold at about $209 and $248 a night, weeks ahead. The similar homes still open on those weekends asked a median of $1,618 and $1,010 for the stay. Homes left open late tend to be the pricier ones, so that gap overstates it. Still, it told us our weekend base price was too low. We raised 23 future weekend nights that day, and each one was read back and locked so no automatic discount could undo it.

What drives demand in Fort Worth

  • The Fort Worth Stock Show and Rodeo. The 2027 show runs January 15 to February 6, 23 days, at the Will Rogers Memorial Center and Dickies Arena.
  • Dickies Arena. It seats up to 14,000 for concerts and 9,300 for rodeo. Some fall weekends have two or three shows.
  • Will Rogers Memorial Center. The 120 acre complex next to the arena draws more than two million visitors a year for horse shows, livestock shows and expos. The NCHA Futurity, a cutting horse championship in Fort Worth, is scheduled for November 16 to December 12, 2026.
  • TCU. Home football weekends, graduations and family visits.
  • Everyday travel. Work trips, family visits, medical stays and people between homes fill weeknights and longer stays.

Five things that move a Fort Worth home up the table

  1. Event aware pricing. Put the Stock Show, the arena calendar and TCU home games into your prices months ahead, and set floors so automatic discounts cannot slide those nights.
  2. Minimum stays by season. Two or three night minimums on event weekends. One night stays allowed midweek when the calendar is empty.
  3. Fast replies. Weeknight guests book the day before. A reply in minutes wins the booking.
  4. Three channels. Airbnb brought most of our bookings, but Vrbo and Booking.com reach guests Airbnb does not. See Airbnb vs Vrbo vs Booking.com.
  5. Longer stays for winter. Weekly and monthly discounts can fill January and February with work and medical stays.

Questions owners ask

What is a good occupancy rate for a Fort Worth Airbnb?

The data services we checked put the typical Fort Worth listing between 41% and 61% of nights booked. Our 3 bedroom home was booked 61% of nights from August through October 2026. Occupancy alone is not the goal: a home that fills every weeknight at a low price can earn less than one that sells fewer nights at the right price.

How much more do weekends earn in Fort Worth?

At the home our team runs, Friday and Saturday nights averaged $236 from August through October 2026, against $139 for weeknights, after discounts, before cleaning, taxes and platform fees. Event weekends can go much higher. That is why weekend prices should be set early and protected with floors.
