Buffalo River Cabin Income by Season: Month by Month

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

Cabins near the Buffalo National River sell a feeling: bluffs, quiet, stars and a long weekend away. The income comes in waves, and the waves do not always follow the river. This guide walks through the year season by season, with market data for the Jasper area and real numbers from two homes our team runs in the region: a glass house that sleeps 10, and a one bedroom cabin loft next to it. For how we run homes here, see our Buffalo River cabin management page.

Short answer. AirROI's data for the Jasper area puts the average listing at $26,695 a year with 37% of nights booked. October, May and June are the strong months. January, February and September are the weak ones. A distinctive home can beat those averages by a wide margin, and a hot tub is one of the biggest dividing lines in the data.

Spring: March to May

Spring is when the upper river is most reliable. The Buffalo River Chamber's river guide says floating on the upper river around Ponca is mostly limited to spring and early summer, possibly fall if it is a wet one, and depends on rain. Waterfalls run after storms and the trails turn green. May is a peak month in the AirROI data.

At our glass house, a mid March 2027 stay was booked seven months ahead at $449 a night. Spring weekends book early for the same reason fall weekends do: people plan Ozark trips around a known window.

Summer: June to August

June is the area's top revenue month in AirROI's data. Families and big groups travel, and nightly rates peak. But the upper river is a different story by late summer. A park spokesperson told KY3 that in the peak summer season the upper section of the river around Ponca tends to dry up, and that it happens every year.

That matters for how you sell. A listing that promises floating in August will disappoint guests and earn bad reviews. Sell what is always there: the deck, the hot tub, the trails, the view and the dark sky.

Our glass house was booked 16 of 31 nights in August 2026 at an average of $455, mostly by groups of four to eight.

Fall: September to November

Fall is two seasons in one. September is one of the weakest months in the market data. October is one of the strongest.

September. Our glass house filled 25 of 30 nights, but only by selling many weeknights under $200 to people who booked a few days out. The month's average night fell to $306. Full is not the same as profitable. Each of those short stays still needed a full clean and still added wear. On September 28 we set a hard floor of $349 a night, and every night booked since has sold at $314 or more.

October. The leaves turn and the elk rut is on. Arkansas tourism notes that September through October is the elk mating season, when bulls bugle in the open fields of Boxley Valley, and that fall weekends draw crowds to the roadside pullouts. Our glass house has 27 of 31 October nights booked at an average of $502, with Friday and Saturday nights at $685, after discounts, before cleaning, taxes and platform fees. October is its best month so far. Our pricing engine took over its prices on September 21, 2026, and 15 of those 27 nights were booked before then.

November. Quieter, but still strong early in the month. As of mid October, our glass house had 14 November nights booked, or 47%, at an average of $547, including Thanksgiving at $624 a night.

Winter: December to February

January has the lowest occupancy in the AirROI data, and February is the lowest revenue month. Holidays are the exception. Our glass house sold Christmas at $799 a night and the week after at $632 a night. An eight night stay in late January was booked six months ahead at $529 a night. As of mid October, though, January and February were only 16% and 18% booked.

Winter also brings snow, ice and slow gravel roads. Tell guests plainly before they book, so normal winter weather never turns into a cancellation dispute. Elk can still be seen at dawn and dusk, and clear cold nights give the best stargazing of the year. A hot tub turns a cold weekend into a selling point.

A small cabin, month by month

Our one bedroom cabin loft sleeps 4 and went live on September 24, 2026. As a new listing with no reviews, we priced weeknights at $89 and weekends at $149 until it earns its first three reviews. In October it has 16 of 31 nights booked, or 52%, at an average of $97: $113 on weekend nights and $87 on weeknights. October payouts come to $1,709. Five habits that smooth out the seasons

  1. Price weekends early. At our glass house, stays that included a weekend night were booked a median of 45 days ahead. Weekday only stays were booked a median of 5 days ahead.
  2. Hold floors in slow months. Decide your lowest price before the slow month starts. A cheap weeknight earns little once platform fees, utilities and wear are paid.
  3. Use minimum stays for holidays. Both holiday weeks at our glass house sold with three night minimums.
  4. Write for rural arrivals. Weak cell signal, gravel roads and steep drives need clear directions, photos of where to park, and plain advice on what vehicle to bring.
  5. Sell what is always there. Views, privacy, the hot tub, trails and stars. The river is a bonus when the water is up.

Questions owners ask

What is the best month for a Buffalo River cabin?

October, May and June are the peak months in AirROI's data for the Jasper area. At our glass house, October 2026 is the best month so far, with 27 of 31 nights booked as of October 16 at an average of $502.

Can I count on floating to fill summer weekends?

No. The upper river around Ponca tends to dry up in peak summer, according to the park. Sell the home, the views and the amenities, and treat good water as a bonus.

How do cabins near the Buffalo River do in winter?

January and February are the slowest months in the market data. Holidays sell well, and a hot tub helps. Plan for a thin calendar and be clear with guests about winter roads.
