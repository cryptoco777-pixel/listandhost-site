Airbnb Management Fees in 2026: What Is Normal and What Is Included

List & Host team · September 29, 2026

The first question every owner asks a manager is the fee. The better question is what the fee covers and what it is calculated on. Two quotes that look far apart can cost the same once the extras are added up, and two quotes with the same percentage can be thousands of dollars apart. This guide shows the usual ranges, the three bases a percentage can be taken from, a worked comparison and the contract terms to read before you sign. Typical fee ranges

Type of managementTypical fee

These ranges come from public industry guides, listed at the bottom of this page. Local quotes vary. Full service is what most owners want: listing, pricing, guest messaging, cleaning coordination and maintenance, all handled. Remote and co hosting plans cost less because the owner, or a local person the owner hires, handles anything that needs hands at the home.

What the percentage is taken from

This is where most owners get surprised. A percentage needs a base, and managers use different ones. Here is one example year, used in every table below. It is an illustration, not a forecast for any home:

  • Guests pay $40,000 in nightly rent over the year.
  • Guests also pay $6,000 in cleaning fees, which go to the cleaners.
  • The platform keeps 15.5% of the nightly rent as its host fee: $6,200.
What the 20% is taken fromBase in the exampleFee for the year

Nightly rent before platform fees$40,000$8,000
Nightly rent after the platform’s host fee$33,800$6,760

Same percentage, $2,440 apart. Before you compare rates, ask each manager to write down the base in one sentence. If the fee comes out of cleaning fees, the cleaner or the owner pays for it in the end.

What is usually included

  • Creating and optimizing listings on the major platforms
  • Daily pricing and calendar management
  • Guest communication before, during and after each stay
  • Booking management and payment handling
  • Scheduling cleaners around every checkout
  • A monthly statement with every booking, fee and cost

What is often billed on top

  • Cleaning: usually $50 to $350 or more per turnover, often covered by the guest cleaning fee.
  • Maintenance: the vendor cost, sometimes plus a 5% to 15% coordination markup.
  • Supplies: toiletries, paper goods, linens and restocking.
  • Recurring services: lawn, pool or hot tub care.
  • Setup fees: anywhere from $0 to $1,000.
  • Photography and furnishing: sometimes billed at cost, sometimes marked up. Ask which.

How to compare two quotes

Take a realistic year of bookings for your home and run both quotes against it, including every extra. A lower percentage with markups on repairs and supplies can end up costing more than a flat 20%. Contract terms to read before you sign

The fee is one line. The agreement decides how easy the relationship is to live with, and how easy it is to leave. Read these parts closely:

  • Term and notice. How long is the first term, and how many days of notice end it? Is there a fee to leave early?
  • Who owns the listing. If the listing sits in your own account, the reviews and booking history stay with you. If it sits in the manager’s account, they usually stay with the manager.
  • Where payouts go. Straight to you from the platforms, or through the manager first? Either can work, but you should get a statement that ties every payout to a booking.
  • Repair approvals. Above what amount does the manager need your yes? Is there a written quote first?
  • Bookings after you leave. Who hosts stays that were booked before the end date, and who is paid for them?
  • Owner use. Can you block dates for yourself, and does the manager charge anything for nights you use?
  • Statement timing. When does the monthly statement arrive, and what does it show?

Red flags

  • A percentage with no written base.
  • Markups on repairs or supplies that are not on the statement.
  • A long first term with a large early exit fee.
  • No monthly statement, or one that shows totals but not each booking.
  • Pricing that never changes. A manager should be moving rates for seasons, weekends and local events.

Questions owners ask about fees

What is a normal Airbnb management fee?

Full service usually runs 18% to 25% of booking revenue. Remote and co hosting plans often run 12% to 18%. Luxury and resort markets can run higher. Always ask what the percentage is taken from.

Is the management fee charged on cleaning fees?

It depends on the contract. Some managers take their percentage from everything guests pay, cleaning included. Ours is not: booking revenue is the nightly rent plus any extra guest fees, after the platform’s host fee, and leaves out taxes and cleaning fees.

Can I switch managers without losing my reviews?

Yes, if the listing is in your own account. The reviews stay with the listing, and the new manager joins it as your host team. If the old manager’s account holds the listing, the reviews usually stay with them, so check this before you sign.
