Hot Tub Rentals: Upkeep, Costs and What a Tub Earns

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

Few upgrades change a short term rental like a hot tub. Guests search for it, photos sell it, and in cabin markets it can be the difference between a full winter and an empty one. It also adds work at every single turnover. Here is what a tub can earn, what it costs to run, and the routine that keeps guests safe. We run a hot tub at our glass house in the Buffalo River region and are adding one at our Fort Worth home this winter.

Do guests really want it?

Yes. Airbnb's own guide to the amenities guests search for most lists the hot tub sixth, ahead of a washer or dryer, self check in and a grill. In cabin markets it matters even more. In AirROI's data for the Jasper area near the Buffalo National River, 27.8% of listings have a hot tub. Those listings averaged $43,474 a year in revenue, against $20,242 for listings without one.

That gap is not all the tub. Homes with tubs are often bigger and better equipped. Beyond Pricing, a pricing company, reported in October 2026 that hosts in markets such as Atlanta, Oahu, Los Angeles and the North Carolina mountains can charge a 20% to 25% premium for listings with a hot tub. It did not publish its method in detail, so treat that as a direction, not a guarantee.

Our own experience

The glass house our team runs in the Buffalo River region has a hot tub. It is rated 4.9 on Airbnb and is a Guest Favorite. For October 2026 it has 27 of 31 nights booked as of October 16, at an average of $502 a night. The tub is one reason among many, along with the views, the space and the design, so we do not credit it with a share of that number. See the glass house results page for the full picture.

At our Fort Worth home we ordered an entry level 6 to 7 person spa for $3,599.99 before tax, and budgeted about $5,000 all in with wiring and placement. We will add its results to this guide once it has a season of bookings.

Rental tubs run harder than home tubs. Guests leave the cover off, soak longer and want it hot on arrival. Plan for the electricity line to run higher than a home example, especially in winter.

The turnover routine

A home hot tub might see a few soaks a week. A rental tub can see six adults a night, every night. Treat it closer to a public spa than a backyard one.

  1. Test at every turnover. Check sanitizer and pH before each new guest, not once a week. For public hot tubs, the CDC's targets are 3 to 10 parts per million of free chlorine or 4 to 8 of bromine, and a pH of 7.2 to 7.8, tested at least twice a day. Follow your tub maker's limits too.
  2. Shock, rinse and skim. Shock after each stay, rinse or swap the filter, and clear debris from the water and the cover.
  3. Drain on a schedule. Most home spa manuals call for a full drain every few months, sooner with heavy use. A rental counts as heavy use. Put a fixed drain date on your cleaning calendar.
  4. Drain at once after contamination. Vomit, fecal matter, glass or bath bombs mean drain, clean and keep the tub closed until the water is balanced again. Do not shock it and hope.
  5. Log every reading. Write down pH, sanitizer and temperature at each turnover. A log protects you if a guest ever raises a health complaint.
  6. Set a safe temperature. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission says hot tub water should never exceed 104 degrees Fahrenheit, and advises pregnant women to stay at 100 degrees or below. Post that in the house rules.
  7. Cover and lock. A locking cover protects children, keeps debris out and holds heat.

When a tub is not worth it

  • Your electrical service cannot carry another 240 volt load without an expensive upgrade.
  • There is no private, level spot that neighbors cannot see into.
  • Your cleaner cannot take on tub care, and no service covers your area.
  • Comparable homes in your market rarely have tubs and guests do not filter for one. Try the hot tub filter on a search for your area first.
  • Your insurance will not cover it, or the premium jump eats the gain. Ask your agent before you buy.

Listing it right

  • Put the tub in the first five photos, with the view if you have one.
  • Name it in the title if there is room. Guests search for it.
  • Post clear rules: no glass, shower first, no children without an adult, and the set temperature.
  • If the tub is ever down, tell guests before they arrive and offer a fair fix. A surprise broken tub is one of the fastest ways to a bad review.

Questions owners ask

Does a hot tub increase Airbnb bookings?

Guests search for it: Airbnb lists the hot tub among the ten amenities guests search for most. In AirROI's Jasper area data, listings with a tub averaged $43,474 a year against $20,242 without, though tub homes are often larger. It is one of the strongest amenities in cabin markets.

How often should a rental hot tub be drained?

Most home spa manuals call for a full drain every few months, sooner with heavy use, and a rental is heavy use. Test the water at every turnover, and drain at once after any contamination.

How much does it cost to run a hot tub?

In Hydropool's example a tub uses about 187 kWh a month, which is about $27 to $30 at July 2026 average home electricity rates in Arkansas and Texas. Rental use runs higher. Add chemicals, filters, cleaner time and any service visits.
