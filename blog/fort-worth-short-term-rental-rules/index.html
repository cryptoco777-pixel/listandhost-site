Fort Worth Short Term Rental Rules: What Owners Need to Know

List & Host team · September 29, 2026

Fort Worth is one of the strongest short term rental markets in Texas, with Dickies Arena, the Stock Show, TCU and the Stockyards pulling visitors all year. It also has some of the clearest rules in the area about where rentals can operate. Before you list or buy, here is what you need to know.

Where short term rentals are allowed

Under the city's ordinance, short term rentals are allowed in mixed use districts and most form based, commercial and industrial zoning districts. They are not allowed in residential zoning districts, including the one family and two family districts that cover most neighborhoods.

In March 2025 a district court judge ruled that the city can keep enforcing that ordinance, rejecting a challenge from rental operators. Operators said they planned to appeal, so it is worth watching, but for now the rule stands.

Check zoning first. Before anything else, confirm your property's zoning district. The city's registration process asks for a zoning confirmation, and it is the first thing we check for every Fort Worth owner.

Registration

  • Owners register each year before listing, through the city's online system.
  • The first year application fee is $150, and renewals are $100 a year.
  • The application includes a zoning confirmation for the address.

Hotel occupancy tax

Fort Worth charges a 9% hotel occupancy tax on room receipts: 7% city hotel tax plus 2% convention tax. Returns are filed monthly and due by the 25th of the following month, and late filings carry a penalty of 15% of the tax owed. Texas also has a state hotel occupancy tax, so make sure both the city and state are covered.

What this means if you own in Fort Worth

  • Homes in mixed use or commercial districts, including many townhomes and newer developments near the city's growth areas, can be excellent short term rentals.
  • Homes in single family neighborhoods may be better suited to stays of 30 nights or more, such as traveling nurses and work trips. Ask a local attorney how the ordinance applies to your specific plan.
  • Either way, keep registration, tax filings and records clean. It protects your income and your neighbors' trust.

Rules change, and this guide is general information, not legal advice. Always confirm with the City of Fort Worth before you list.
