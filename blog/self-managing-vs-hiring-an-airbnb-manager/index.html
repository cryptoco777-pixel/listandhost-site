Self Managing vs Hiring a Manager: Honest Cost and Time

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

We are a management company, so read this knowing we have a stake in the answer. Our team also runs three homes, so we know what the work looks like day to day. Some owners should manage their own rental. Others are paying for it with time they do not have. This guide lays out the hours, the dollars and the break even point so you can decide with real numbers. How much time self managing takes

PriceLabs surveyed more than 1,400 hosts for its Global Host Report 2025. They spent an average of 8.3 hours a week managing their listings, which is about 36 hours a month. 76% said admin work such as bookkeeping and taxes was highly time consuming, and 72% said the same about cleaning and maintenance.

Here is our own estimate for one home with about eight stays a month, close to what our Fort Worth home booked in September and October 2026.

Basis: our own operating experience across three homes, checked against the PriceLabs survey average. These are estimates. Your hours depend on how far you live from the home, how many stays you get and how much you automate.

The hours are not the hardest part. The timing is. At our Fort Worth home, stays with only weeknights were booked a median of one day before arrival. Guests message at night, on holidays and during your own vacation. Turnovers happen in the five hours between an 11 AM checkout and a 4 PM check in. Self managing means being reachable for all of it, or paying someone to be.

What doing it well looks like

Pricing alone is a daily job if it is done right. In the first 26 days our engine ran live, from September 21 to October 16, 2026, it logged 133 nightly price changes and 52 minimum stay changes across the three homes our team runs, all written and read back. Each one was checked against a floor, written, read back and logged. Even so, our October 13 comp check found open dates at our Fort Worth home priced 11% to 25% under the local median, and we fixed them that day. The money is in the details: event floors, minimum stays, one night gaps and discounts that stack. You can see the moves behind our numbers on our results page.

What a manager costs

Full service managers commonly charge 18% to 25% of booking revenue. Some add setup fees, markups on repairs and supplies, or take the percentage from a different base. Read our guide to management fees before you compare quotes, and see our pricing for what we charge.

On this example, the $8,000 fee buys back about 200 to 450 hours a year. That works out to roughly $18 to $40 for each hour you no longer spend. If your time is worth more than that, or you simply do not have those hours, a manager can make sense before you count any change in revenue. If you have the time and enjoy the work, self managing keeps that money in your pocket.

The revenue question

A good manager should earn back part of the fee through better pricing, more channels and better reviews. A weak one will not. Nobody can promise you a number, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Ask any manager how often they check comps, who sets the floors, and what they do when a weekend is still open two weeks out. Ask to see a real monthly statement from a home they run.

The middle options

It does not have to be all or nothing.

  • Co hosting. You keep the listing in your name, and your reviews stay yours. A co host handles messages, pricing and cleaning coordination. You or a local helper handle anything that needs hands at the home.
  • A launch package. One time help to set up the home, listing, photos, pricing, lock and cleaner. Then you run it.
  • Remote pricing and listing work. You handle guests and cleaning. Someone else runs daily pricing and keeps the listing sharp. This works in any market, because it needs no local team.

List & Host offers all three alongside full service management. The services page explains each one.

Who should self manage

  • You live within 20 to 30 minutes of the home.
  • You have one home and flexible hours.
  • You enjoy hosting and answer messages fast, even at night.
  • You have a reliable cleaner and a backup.

Who should get help

  • You live far away or travel often.
  • You work a full time job.
  • You own two or more homes, or plan to.
  • Your market runs on events, where pricing mistakes cost the most.
  • You want the income without being on call.

Questions to ask yourself before you decide

  1. How many hours a month can I honestly give this, including nights and weekends?
  2. Who covers for me when I am sick or away?
  3. What is one hour of my time worth?
  4. If I hire, what is the fee charged on, and what is billed on top?
  5. How do I leave the agreement if it is not working?

Questions owners ask

How many hours a week does it take to self manage an Airbnb?

PriceLabs' 2025 survey of more than 1,400 hosts found an average of 8.3 hours a week. Our own estimate for one home with about eight stays a month is 17 to 38 hours a month, depending on distance and automation.

Is hiring an Airbnb manager worth it?

On a home with $40,000 a year in nightly revenue, a 20% fee is $8,000 and buys back roughly 200 to 450 hours. That is about $18 to $40 an hour. If your time is worth more, or you do not have the hours, it often is.

What is the difference between co hosting and full service management?

With co hosting you keep the listing and your reviews, and the co host handles messages, pricing and cleaning coordination. Full service management also covers local cleaning, maintenance and everything that needs hands at the home.
