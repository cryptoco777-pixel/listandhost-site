Dynamic Pricing Explained for Short Term Rental Owners

List & Host team · October 16, 2026

A short term rental sells a different product every night. A Saturday during a big event and a Tuesday in January are not the same night, and pricing them the same leaves money on the table on one and an empty bed on the other. Dynamic pricing means moving the nightly price, and the minimum stay, with demand. This guide explains how it works in plain terms, using the rules our engine runs every day on the homes our team runs in Fort Worth and the Buffalo River region. We offer the same engine to owners in other markets through our remote pricing service.

Why one price all year loses money

Three numbers from our own booking records, for stays from July to October 2026, show why a fixed price cannot work:

  • Weekends are worth more. At our Fort Worth home, Friday and Saturday nights averaged $233 and weeknights $143, after discounts, before cleaning, taxes and platform fees. At our glass house, Friday and Saturday nights averaged $535 and weeknights $366, on the same basis. A stay with no nightly split in our records counts at its average night.
  • Event weekends are worth more again. A three night stay over the Red River weekend in October averaged $254 a night at our Fort Worth home, about 50% above that home's $169 average for the month.
  • Many guests book late. Of 75 stays booked across the three homes our team runs from July to mid October 2026, 25 were booked less than a week before arrival. A price set months ago and never touched again is wrong for those nights in one direction or the other.

The parts of a pricing system

Every good pricing system, ours or anyone's, does the same few jobs. Here is each one, with the rule we actually run.

1. A floor under every night

The floor is the lowest nightly price any automatic rule may set. It protects the value of the home. Our Fort Worth home's floor is $149 and our glass house floor is $349. No automatic rule ever writes a price below the floor. Platform discounts you choose, like a last minute discount, still come off the nightly price after the fact, so set those knowing where your floor sits.

2. Event weeks, written down in advance

We keep a dated list of the events that move demand in each market, taken from official calendars: college football, concerts at Dickies Arena, Cowboys games, holidays and the Fort Worth Stock Show and Rodeo. Each event gets its own floor and its own minimum stay. Two real entries:

  • Stock Show, January 15 to February 6, 2027: floors of $299 on weeknights and $379 on weekends, with a two night minimum.
  • Red River weekend in October: a three night minimum, so one night bookings cannot break up the weekend.

Event floors are guards against cutting too far. The engine never raises one on its own. If the market for an event is hotter than the floor, a person decides the raise.

3. Weekend minimums that know about gaps

Friday and Saturday nights carry a two night minimum, so a single Saturday booking does not strand the Friday. The exception is a gap. If the open stretch is only one night long, the minimum stays at one, because a two night rule would make that night impossible to book.

4. Gap nights

A gap night is one open night stuck between two bookings. A home with a blanket two night minimum loses these all year. Our engine finds every one night gap and sets its minimum stay to one night. Short openings sell when the minimum allows it: in October 2026 a two night opening at our Fort Worth home, October 7 and 8, sold as two separate one night stays, both booked on October 7, between a stay that left on the 7th and one that arrived on the 9th.

5. The fill ladder

Open nights get cheaper slowly as the date gets closer, inside a fixed window, and never below the floor. The rules:

  • The ladder works only on open stretches inside a window: 22 to 35 days out for our Fort Worth home and 15 to 29 days out for our glass house.
  • Each night can be cut once a week, by 10%.
  • No night ever goes more than 20% below the price it had when the engine first saw it, and never below the floor.
  • A Friday or Saturday is never priced below a weeknight in the same week.
  • Nights with a minimum stay of three or more, usually event nights, are never cut.
  • The window ends where the platform's own last minute discounts begin, at 21 days for Fort Worth and 14 days for the glass house, so two discounts never stack on the same night.

A real example: on September 21, 2026, the ladder cut three open Fort Worth weeknights, October 13 to 15, from $179 to $161, at 22 to 24 days out. Those nights later sold as part of a four night stay.

6. Watching for raises

Cutting is only half the job. When most of a week is already booked, the open nights left in it are worth more, not less. Our engine flags those weeks and proposes a specific raise for the open nights. A person approves each raise before it is written, and approved nights are put on hold so the fill ladder cannot cut them back down.

7. Safety on every change

  • The engine never touches a booked night.
  • Every change is logged before it is sent, then read back from the calendar to confirm it held. A mismatch sends an alert.
  • Each run has a cap on how many nights it may change. A price run that would change more writes nothing and reports it.
  • Nights a person sets by hand can be put on hold, and no automatic rule touches a held night.

What about Airbnb Smart Pricing?

Airbnb's Smart Pricing adjusts your nightly price based on demand, using what Airbnb describes as hundreds of factors about your listing and your area, inside a minimum and maximum you set. It is a fair starting point. Know its limits before you rely on it:

  • Airbnb says the price a guest pays can go below the minimum you set, because discounts still apply.
  • Weekly, monthly and trip length discounts override Smart Pricing.
  • It prices one platform. If you also list on Vrbo and Booking.com, each needs its own prices.
  • It does not know your cleaning cost, your event calendar or the nights you would rather leave empty than sell cheap.

Questions to ask any manager about pricing

  1. What is my floor, and who can change it?
  2. How do you price event weeks, and where does your event list come from?
  3. Do you sell one night gaps?
  4. When do open nights start getting cheaper, by how much, and what stops them?
  5. Do your cuts stack with the platform's last minute discounts?
  6. Who approves a raise, and how fast?
  7. Can I see every price change you made last month?

Quick answers

What is a price floor?

The lowest nightly price any automatic rule may set. Our Fort Worth home's floor is $149 and our glass house floor is $349.

What is a gap night?

An open night stuck between two bookings. A blanket two night minimum makes it impossible to book, so our engine sets the minimum stay for those gaps to one night.

Does Airbnb Smart Pricing do the same thing?

Smart Pricing adjusts nightly prices inside a minimum and maximum you set. Airbnb notes that discounts can still take the price a guest pays below your minimum, and it prices only Airbnb.
