Compare: solo co host

A solo co host or full service: what each one actually covers

A good co host can be a great deal: one person who knows your home, answers fast and often costs less than a company. The gaps show up when that person is sick or away, or when something at the home needs hands. Here is the line by line view.

Checked October 17, 2026. We are a management company, so we have a stake in your answer. Every outside number names its source, listed at the bottom of the page, and every number of ours says what it is based on.

The basics

What a co host is

A co host is someone you add to your listing to help run it. On Airbnb, the owner picks the access level: full access (calendar, pricing, listing details and reservations), calendar and messaging, or calendar only. Only the listing owner can set up or change a co host's payout. You keep the listing, the reviews and the payouts (Airbnb Help Center).

Airbnb launched its Co-Host Network in October 2024 to help owners find local co hosts, and said those co hosts averaged 4.86 stars (Airbnb Newsroom). Fees are agreed between owner and co host. A solo co host can be excellent. The question is what one person can cover.

The honest picks

Which one fits your home

A solo co host is usually better if

  • You already have one who answers fast, knows your home and has a backup
  • You live nearby, or have a handy person for repairs and a cleaner you trust
  • You only list on Airbnb and do not want other channels
  • You want the lowest cost and are fine filling the gaps yourself

Full service is usually better if

  • You live far away and nobody near the home can handle a lockout or a leak
  • Your co host is one person and you have had gaps when they were away
  • You want Airbnb, Vrbo and Booking.com on one calendar
  • You want one monthly statement with every booking and every cost

There is a middle path. We also offer co hosting: you keep your listing and your own cleaner, and we run pricing, guest messages, calendars and reviews, at a price quoted in writing after a free look. Details are on the services page.

Before you add anyone

Questions to ask a co host

  1. Who answers my guests when you are asleep, sick or on vacation?
  2. How often do you check my prices against nearby homes, and with what?
  3. Who schedules and checks the cleaner?
  4. Who goes to the home for a lockout, a leak or a broken hot tub?
  5. What exactly is your fee charged on, and what access do you need?

Skip us if

  • You have a co host you trust who answers fast and has a backup. Keep them. If pricing is the weak spot, look at remote pricing on the services page.
  • You only want help with guest messages and handle everything else yourself.
  • You need someone local for hands at the home outside our area. Full service in Fort Worth, the rest of Dallas Fort Worth and the Buffalo River region of Arkansas. Co hosting or remote pricing anywhere else in the United States.

Owner questions

Questions owners ask

What is the difference between a co host and a full service manager?

A co host joins your listing with the access you choose, and you keep the listing, the reviews and the payouts. A full service manager also runs everything at the home: cleaners, repairs and supplies, usually on every channel. With us, platform payouts still go straight to you.

How much does an Airbnb co host charge?

There is no set rate. Owner and co host agree on it. One industry glossary puts typical co host pay at 10% to 30% of booking revenue, higher for co hosts who also handle cleaning and maintenance.

Is a solo co host enough for one home?

Often, yes, if they answer fast, live near the home or have local help, and have someone covering when they are away. The risk is one person: when they are out, the guest still needs an answer.

Does List & Host offer co hosting too?

Yes. You keep your listing and your own cleaner, and we run pricing, guest messages, calendars and reviews. The price is quoted in writing after a free look.
