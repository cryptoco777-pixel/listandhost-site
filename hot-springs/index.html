Hot Springs vacation rental pricing, from lake houses to Bathhouse Row

Updated October 18, 2026

Hot Springs sells two trips. A lake weekend on Hamilton, Ouachita or Catherine. And a town stay near Bathhouse Row, Central Avenue and Oaklawn. We offer remote pricing and listing work to Hot Springs owners with the same engine that prices the Arkansas homes our team runs. You keep your local cleaner.

Short answer

The average Hot Springs, AR short term rental listed at $265 a night, booked 34% of its nights and took in about $27,407 in yearly revenue (AirROI public market data, Aug 2025 to Jul 2026, read October 16, 2026). A market average is not a promise of what your home will earn. We do not run full service here yet. Co hosting or remote pricing does. The sources agree on rate: the mid to high $200s a night. They disagree on occupancy, from about a third of nights to nearly half. That gap is the difference between a listing that is priced and run every day and one that is not.

Guests

Two kinds of Hot Springs guest

Lake guests

  • Waterfront, a dock and room to park a boat.
  • Sleeps 8 or more for family weekends.
  • Kayaks, a fire pit and a deck at sunset in the first photos.

Town guests

  • A walk to Bathhouse Row and Central Avenue.
  • Minutes to Oaklawn on race days.
  • Couples looking for a spa weekend, with easy parking.

What moves the rate

  • A hot tub: $52,713 a year on average with one, $21,316 without. Only 19.4% of listings have one.
  • A sauna: $71,257 with, in 1.3% of listings.
  • Life size games: $80,022 with, in 2.4% of listings.

Compare Broken Bow, where 86% of cabins have a hot tub and it no longer sets a home apart. In Hot Springs it still does.

AirROI compares listings with and without each feature, August 2025 to July 2026. It shows correlation, not a promise.

How the pricing engine works

  • A rate for every night, a year out, with a floor you approve. No price we set goes under it.
  • Event nights priced on their own, set before the crowd books.
  • Minimum stays by season, lowered for short gaps between stays so those nights can still sell.
  • A fill ladder: unsold nights step down on a set schedule as the date gets close, never past the floor.
  • Every change logged and read back from the channel, so what you see is what guests see.
